==================================================================
 BAMBU A1 PLOTTER — INSTRUCTIONS
 Image / SVG -> pen plotter G-code, for the Bambu Lab A1 ONLY
==================================================================

This tool converts images and SVG files into G-code that turns your
Bambu Lab A1 into a pen plotter, using a pen held in a mount fitted
to the FRONT of the toolhead — the nozzle stays where it is and is
never removed. It runs entirely in your browser: nothing you load is
ever uploaded anywhere. No install, no account, and no internet
connection needed once the file is downloaded.

What is included: raster image tracing, single-colour SVG import and
the full set of machine settings. Not included: multi-colour layer
separation with hatch fill, plotted text, QR codes, frame guides and
ASCII art.


------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. WARNINGS — READ THIS FIRST
------------------------------------------------------------------

  USE AT YOUR OWN RISK.

  This tool generates machine motion commands for a 3D printer being
  used outside its intended purpose. Incorrect configuration can
  cause the toolhead to crash, drag across the bed, or damage the
  printer, the pen mount, or the print surface.

  - This tool is built and tested for the BAMBU LAB A1 ONLY.
    It is NOT verified on the A1 Mini, P1P, P1S, X1 Carbon, or any
    other printer. Those machines use different bed sizes, different
    motion systems (some move the toolhead, others move the bed),
    and may behave differently when paused or homed. Do not assume
    this G-code is safe on any printer other than the A1.

  - Always let the SETUP HOLD and BOUNDARY CHECK finish before
    walking away from a plot (see Section 5). These exist so you can
    catch a mistake before the pen starts drawing.

  - Always verify your printer's nozzle is set to 0.4mm in both the
    printer's own settings and in Bambu Studio's active printer
    profile. The G-code in this tool is generated assuming a 0.4mm
    nozzle profile — a mismatch can cause Bambu Studio or the
    printer to reject the file, or behave unpredictably.

  - Never let the nozzle preheat. This tool commands heaters off,
    but if you manually preheat the nozzle before running a plot,
    remove that filament and heat source first. This is a pen
    plotter conversion — there should be no filament loaded and no
    reason for heat at any point in this process.

  - No liability is accepted for damage to your printer,
    your workspace, or any injury arising from use of this tool.
    You are responsible for supervising your printer during use.


------------------------------------------------------------------
 2. WHAT YOU NEED
------------------------------------------------------------------

  - A Bambu Lab A1 printer
  - A pen mount for the FRONT of the toolhead (see Section 7 for
    what makes a good one). You fit the pen during the setup hold,
    not before the plot starts — see Section 5.
  - Bambu Studio, for sending the G-code to your printer
  - Any modern browser (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge)
  - The printer's nozzle set to 0.4mm, both on the printer itself
    and in Bambu Studio's active printer profile
  - Paper taped down firmly on all four sides


------------------------------------------------------------------
 3. THE INTERFACE
------------------------------------------------------------------

  The tool is a single page: a settings sidebar on the left, and a
  preview/output area on the right. Every sidebar section is
  collapsed when the page loads — click a section's title to open
  it.

  SOURCE
    Drop an image (JPG, PNG, WEBP) or an SVG file, or click to
    browse. Images can be rotated 90 degrees either way before
    tracing. SVG paths are sampled directly and plotted as a single
    colour — per-colour layer separation is not included. "Remove
    image" clears the source.

  TRACE
    Converts a raster image (JPG/PNG/WEBP) into plotted linework.
    Three modes:
      - Sketch: traces edges into clean lines.
      - Halftone: converts tone into a grain or dot pattern.
      - Lo-fi: traces at reduced resolution for a chunky, stepped
        look.
    Each mode exposes different sliders, also mirrored as a two-
    slider quick strip under the Vector preview for fast tweaking
    without opening the sidebar. Brightness, Contrast, and Light
    Image Boost (auto-contrast) adjust the source image before
    tracing; Invert is for tracing light-on-dark images.

  BED & PLACEMENT
    Bed size and how the artwork is placed on it (Centered,
    Anchored to a corner, or Custom), plus margins.

  PEN (Z-LIFT)
    Pen Down Z, Pen Up (safe) Z, the Setup/swap hold time, and the
    Pen Y Offset — see Section 4, First-Time Setup, for how to
    measure and set these correctly before your first plot.

  VECTOR PREVIEW / DRAW SIMULATION / G-CODE
    Three tabs above the main preview:
      - Vector preview: a fixed, true-to-scale view of the plate
        with your artwork at its real position.
      - Draw simulation: the same view, animated to show the order
        strokes will be drawn in — use Play/Pause, the scrub bar,
        and the speed slider to step through it.
      - G-code: the generated file, with Download and Copy buttons.
    Both preview tabs have zoom controls (−, percentage, +, Reset)
    above the panel — zoom in for detail work. If the zoomed view
    is larger than your window, scroll to reach the rest of it.


------------------------------------------------------------------
 4. FIRST-TIME SETUP (do this once)
------------------------------------------------------------------

  The key idea: you do NOT fit the pen before starting a plot. The
  printer homes first (which touches the nozzle down to the plate),
  and only then pauses with the toolhead in position for you to fit
  and calibrate the pen. This keeps the pen well clear of the
  homing routine. See Section 5 for the full sequence.

  What to set up once, before your first plot:

  1. Fit your pen HOLDER (empty, no pen yet, or with the pen
     retracted) to the FRONT of the toolhead, so that when a pen is
     installed its tip will sit forward of where the nozzle points
     down. This forward position is what the tool's coordinate math
     is built around.

  2. Measure the distance from the nozzle tip to where your pen tip
     will sit, along the Y axis only (front-to-back). This distance
     is your PEN Y OFFSET. The tool defaults to 48mm — this is very
     likely wrong for your specific mount and pen. Measure your own
     setup; don't rely on the default.

  3. Open the tool in your browser. Under Bed & Placement, enter
     your measured Pen Y Offset.

  4. Under Pen (Z-lift), set Pen Down Z to roughly where the pen
     tip should sit to touch the paper. This just needs to be in
     the right ballpark — you fine-tune the actual contact by
     adjusting the pen in its mount during the hold, not by
     perfecting this number.

  5. Confirm your printer and Bambu Studio's active profile are
     both set to a 0.4mm nozzle.

  6. Run one test plot on scrap paper first. Use the setup hold to
     fit and calibrate the pen (Section 5), then watch the Boundary
     Check trace to confirm the plot stays on the plate. Only
     proceed to a real piece once both look right.


------------------------------------------------------------------
 5. HOW A PLOT RUNS — WHAT TO EXPECT
------------------------------------------------------------------

  Every plot follows the same sequence:

  1. HOMING — the printer homes all axes (G28). This is mandatory
     and cannot be skipped; it's what keeps the origin accurate.

     The A1 homes Z by touching the nozzle position down to the
     plate. Because you fit the pen AFTER homing (during the hold,
     next step), the pen is nowhere near the plate during this
     touch-off — which is exactly why the workflow is ordered this
     way. Keep the pen out of the mount, or retracted, until the
     hold begins.

  2. SETUP HOLD — the toolhead travels to the FRONT-LEFT corner of
     your artwork, lowers to pen-down height, and holds perfectly
     still for a set time (30 seconds by default). This is when you
     FIT AND CALIBRATE THE PEN:

       - Slide the pen into the mount until its tip just touches
         the paper at the corner.
       - Nip up the mount's clamp so the pen can't move.
       - That's your calibration — the tip is now set to the paper
         surface at a known, repeatable point.

     Because the hold always happens at the same corner at the same
     height, every plot calibrates from an identical reference. If
     the default time is too short to fit the pen comfortably,
     raise it: under Pen (Z-lift), the "Setup / swap hold time"
     slider goes 5-120 seconds. Lower it once you're quick at it.

  3. BOUNDARY CHECK — after the hold, the pen lifts and traces the
     outline of the artwork's bounding box with the pen UP (no
     marks made). Watch this closely. If the head travels toward
     an edge or off the visible plate, STOP the print immediately
     — something is misconfigured (usually the Pen Y Offset).

  4. PLOTTING — the pen lowers and the artwork draws.

     Note: this tool plots a single colour, so there is no
     colour-swap stage — once plotting starts it runs straight
     through to the end without pausing.

------------------------------------------------------------------
 6. THICKER PAPER, CARD, AND OTHER SURFACES
------------------------------------------------------------------

  The tool isn't limited to standard paper. Card, thick watercolour
  stock, or anything raised above the plate simply sits closer to
  the pen, so the pen needs to travel less far down to reach it.

  The control for this is PEN DOWN Z (under Pen (Z-lift)). It sets
  how far the pen lowers on the "pen down" move, measured as a
  height in mm above the build plate (the default of 1mm means the
  toolhead stops 1mm above the plate). Higher values keep the pen
  higher; the thicker your surface, the higher the pen should stop.

  - Thin paper: Pen Down Z near your normal calibrated value.
  - Thicker card / stock: increase Pen Down Z so the pen stops
    higher, since the surface top is now closer to the pen.
  - Very thick or uneven surfaces: increase it further, and lean on
    a spring mount (Section 7) to absorb the variation across the
    sheet.

  You don't have to get this exact. Pen Down Z just needs to be in
  the right ballpark — the real contact is still set by sliding the
  pen in its mount during the setup hold, exactly as with normal
  paper. Set Pen Down Z for roughly the right height, then fine-tune
  the tip against the surface when the hold begins.

  Whatever the surface, always tape it down flat and firmly on all
  sides. Card can bow or lift as the pen drags across it, and any
  movement ruins registration.

------------------------------------------------------------------
 7. PEN HOLDER / MOUNT — WHAT MAKES A GOOD ONE
------------------------------------------------------------------

  The quality of your physical pen mount matters as much as the
  software settings. A few qualities to look for or build toward:

  - FRONT-MOUNTED (attached ahead of the nozzle, not replacing it)
    The pen holder attaches to the toolhead in front of the nozzle
    — the nozzle stays in place, the pen sits forward of it. This
    is what this tool's whole coordinate model assumes (the "Pen Y
    Offset" setting). A clean front-mounted position keeps that
    offset simple, consistent, and small.

  - A SPRING MECHANISM
    A spring-loaded holder lets the pen tip float slightly rather
    than being locked rigidly to the toolhead. Its main benefit is
    compensating for small inconsistencies in paper thickness and
    bed flatness across a plot, keeping line pressure consistent
    without needing a perfect Z number. It also gives you a little
    forgiveness when you set the tip against the paper during the
    hold — the spring takes up minor over-travel instead of
    digging the tip in or lifting it off. (Because the pen is
    fitted after homing, the mount no longer needs to survive the
    Z touch-off, but the pressure consistency alone makes a spring
    well worth it.)

  - PENS WITH FRICTION-TOLERANT TIPS
    The pen tip will be dragged sideways across paper repeatedly
    at speed. Fine felt-tips and very soft brush pens wear down or
    fray quickly under this kind of lateral dragging. Look for
    tips built to handle sustained friction — technical fineliners
    (rated for repeated linework), ballpoints, gel pens, and hard
    plastic or metal-nib pens all hold up well. Soft brush-tip
    markers are the least suited to plotting.

  - AN ADJUSTABLE TIGHTNESS / CLAMP
    Pens vary a lot in barrel diameter. A mount with an adjustable
    clamp, grub screw, or friction-fit collar that can be tuned per
    pen means you're not limited to one exact pen model, and you
    can dial in enough grip to stop the pen twisting or slipping
    during fast direction changes without over-tightening and
    cracking a thin barrel.

  If you're 3D printing a mount, these four qualities
  are essential.

------------------------------------------------------------------
 8. TROUBLESHOOTING
------------------------------------------------------------------

  Plot is offset from where the simulation showed it
    -> Your Pen Y Offset is likely wrong. Re-measure the physical
       distance from nozzle position to pen tip.

  Pen won't reach the paper at the hold
    -> Slide the pen further down in its mount until the tip meets
       the paper, then tighten. That's the intended fix — you set
       contact by moving the pen, not by chasing the Pen Down Z
       number. If the pen bottoms out in the mount and still can't
       reach, lower Pen Down Z slightly (bringing the toolhead
       closer to the plate) and re-run.

  Pen presses too hard / drags / digs in at the hold
    -> Back the pen up slightly in the mount before tightening. A
       spring mount gives you some forgiveness here.

  Pen lifts off the paper in some areas of the bed but not others
    -> Your plate is not perfectly flat, and this tool plots as
       though it were. Every printer bed deviates a little, usually
       most toward the back, and a pen has none of the give that
       molten filament does: a few tenths of a millimetre is the
       difference between a solid line and no line at all.

       There is no setting in the tool that fixes this. What helps,
       roughly in order of how much difference it makes:

       - A spring-loaded pen mount (Section 7). This is the real
         answer, and it is what plotters have always used. The
         spring absorbs the variation mechanically, so the pen
         keeps contact across the whole sheet.
       - Plot nearer the centre and front of the plate, where the
         surface is usually flattest. Shrinking the artwork a
         little is often enough.
       - Lower the Pen Down Z slightly (Section 4) so the pen
         presses harder everywhere. This trades one problem for
         another: it will fix the light areas but press harder in
         the areas that were already fine, which can broaden lines
         or splay a soft nib.
       - Run a bed calibration from the printer's own screen. Worth
         doing, but on its own it will not solve this.

  Boundary check trace goes off the visible plate
    -> Stop the print. Check Pen Y Offset and your placement
       setting (Centered / Anchored / Custom) before trying again.

  Nozzle heats up during a park / pause
    -> This is normal and expected. When the A1 performs a firmware
       park, the printer's own built-in routine heats the nozzle —
       this is hardcoded into the printer's firmware and can't be
       switched off from the G-code. It's harmless for pen
       plotting: there's no filament loaded, so nothing extrudes,
       and the tool re-issues heaters-off commands as soon as the
       plot resumes. Just let it do its thing and carry on. (Do not
       manually load filament or leave any in the hotend.)

  Bambu Studio rejects the file or shows a nozzle mismatch
    -> Confirm the nozzle is set to 0.4mm in BOTH the printer's own
       settings and in Bambu Studio's currently active profile.

  A sidebar section won't show its controls
    -> Sections start collapsed when the page loads. Click the
       section's title to open it.

  Vector preview or draw simulation looks too small to work with
    -> Use the −, +, and Reset buttons above the panel to zoom in.
